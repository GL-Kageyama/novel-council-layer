## 📝 Executive Summary

> Ten evaluators convened on this anonymous word-quota parable, and their collision is unusually legible: the form-focused lens calls the telling canonical, while the value-focused lenses rank it among the strongest of its kind. The premise is the story — words as a countable lifetime currency make response literally expensive, and the motif-chain of unanswered words is load-bearing rather than decorative. The sharpest internal tension is geometric: a conventional parable form carrying near-exceptional emotional, thematic, and character effect. Aggregating to a current value of 71 and a hidden potential of 69, the council classifies the work as innovation — with the explicit caveat that the innovation is in the premise and its execution, not in the narrative form, and that the evaluators themselves split five-to-five between current_success and discovery_target.

## 🤝 Consensus

The council converges on the strength of a single, structurally load-bearing premise: the word-quota world makes every silence, response, and sacrifice measurable, and the chain of unanswered words — the mother's 'sorry,' the old woman's 'welcome home,' the daughter's 'goodbye,' the protagonist's 'I'm home' — is the engine of the piece rather than its decoration. Emotional Power (81), Theme Resonance (82), Character Depth (82), Reader Experience (82), World Building (80), and Plot Architecture (78) all read the work as a complete, honest, lingering experience with strict craft and genuine restraint. The recurring reservation is formal: the two-encounter parable armature, the merchant's conveniently timed revelation, and the ring closure are the genre's canonical shape, and the story's innovation lives in its premise and its execution, not in its telling (Narrative Originality 43, Anti-Generic Story Filter 52).

## 🎯 Recommended Actions

1. Position the work as a polished literary-fantasy parable; its competitive edge is emotional and thematic architecture plus restraint, not formal novelty.
2. If the writer wishes to widen the gap between form and effect, break the canonical two-encounter armature once: give the man-at-sea a consequence in the plot, and dramatize the sacrifice in scene rather than in stated numbers.
3. Reconcile the narration's knowledge contradiction at the crux (the merchant reveals the daughter's death; the protagonist is later said not to know) — it is the one mechanical flaw a strict reader will catch.
4. Consider whether the merchant's ownership of the daughter's 'goodbye' should be bought more dearly within the world's own currency, so the reveal lands as a transaction rather than a convenience.
